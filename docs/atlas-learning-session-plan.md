## Teaching

A teaching session starts with an agreed objective and a visible lesson concept
map. The plan starts conceptual: central objects, motivation, relationships and
useful questions. Conversational work then develops selected results and proofs.
The objective/map remain accessible and visible while working, including in the
fullscreen writing workspace. Show the current area and useful next directions;
“discussed” does not imply “understood independently.”

Aim for 30–60 minutes of substantial learning with a coherent conceptual map, a
few worked derivations and useful tangent branches where the learner dug deeper.
This is a pacing target, not a timeout, fixed proof count or automatic completion
criterion. The learner controls scope, help, depth, tangents and finishing.

## Derivations

A standalone or nested derivation has a persistent Start and Finish:

- Start: explicit assumptions, definitions and known results allowed in the proof.
- Finish: a precise target statement or formula, including conditions.

After seeing both, the learner attempts the argument on a fullscreen zoomable
scratchpad with dictation/text. An explicit submission sends the current work and
explanation together. The assistant responds to that attempt with targeted hints,
corrections and explanations. Continue on the same editable scene and retain prior
submissions, help and unresolved notation. A smooth 2–4 substantial exchanges is
a useful target, not a limit or reason to fabricate a completed proof.

## Interaction policy

Neither teaching nor derivation requires intermediate quizzes, prediction prompts,
checkup questions or repeated “ready to continue?” gates. Use substantive dialogue
and the learner's actual reasoning. Clarifying questions remain appropriate for
ambiguous notation, assumptions, intent or a requested direction. Explain directly
when asked; the learner chooses how much help to receive.

The lesson map is the orientation surface. A focused derivation additionally keeps
its Start/Finish visible. Returning from that derivation resumes the surrounding
lesson; a tangent becomes a retained branch, not an unrelated lost chat.

## Finish and retain

An explicit Finish action prepares a review from the actual session:

- A conceptual map covering the lesson objective and explored tangent branches.
- Detailed proposition/argument graphs for the derivations worked through.
- Source/attempt references, assumptions, received assistance and unresolved gaps.

Link these views under stable concepts, reuse existing claims/arguments through
the reviewed owner/Library path, and keep co-premises joint. Provide a coherent
whole-map/argument review and one explicit integration approval; ordinary learning
should not require repetitive per-node approvals. Ambiguous/conflicting identities
remain visible rather than silently merging or creating duplicate mathematics.
Finishing is not a proof certificate or automatic independent-mastery event.

## Practice remains separate

Preserve AGENTS.md §12, including committed checked solutions, attempt/hint/reveal
history, technique recognition and defective-problem handling. Later practice
includes independent whole-proof reconstruction, applications, multiple-concept
combinations, hidden techniques and challenging Putnam/IMC-style problems. Short
FSRS retrieval and longer problem solving retain distinct scheduling/outcomes.
Do not insert these assessment protocols as mandatory stages inside teaching.
